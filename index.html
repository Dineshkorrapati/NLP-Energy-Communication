How we talk about energy matters

We carry out research on natural language processing for energy economics and policy. Our work focuses on how the energy transition is discussed in public debate: which topics receive attention, which risks are emphasised and how attitudes change over time. We combine open, locally run language models with established research frameworks and human validation.

How we work

Local models

Open-weight models run on our own hardware. No data leaves the chair, and fixed settings make every label reproducible.

Categories from theory

Labels come from energy research, such as the energy trilemma, and from our partners’ coding schemes.

Human validation

We compare model labels with manual coding and repeated runs before drawing conclusions.

Research / Politics

Energy risk framing in the Bundestag

How German parties talk about the environmental, geopolitical and social risks of energy, and how this shifts after major events.

About the project

Energy debates are about more than how energy is produced. They are about which risks deserve attention. We classify every Bundestag energy speech from 2013 to 2025 by the risks it raises, following the three sides of the energy trilemma: environmental, geopolitical and social.

Key findings

  • Costs and fairness now lead. Social risk appears in 74% of energy speeches in 2025, up from 46% in 2013–17.
  • Party profiles are stable. Greens and Linke raise environmental risk far more often than CDU/CSU, in every period.
  • Political shocks move the debate. Geopolitical framing jumped after the 2022 gas supply cut, but not after the 2021 price surge.

Research / Media

Energy coverage in public news

Which energy topics the Tagesschau covers, and with what tone.

About the project

Public broadcasting shapes how most people first hear about energy policy. We analyse energy-related Tagesschau stories sentence by sentence, group them into topics and track the sentiment of the coverage over time.

Sentiment model

Each sentence is scored by three pretrained language models. A logistic regression then combines their outputs into one label: positive, neutral or negative.

Preliminary results

A first run tracks monthly sentiment for stories on energy policy and energy costs from mid-2023 to early 2025.

Research / Industry

Interviews on industrial decarbonisation

Which themes energy-intensive companies raise about decarbonisation, and how they feel about them.

About the project

Together with KEI, we analyse interviews with energy-intensive companies. The interviews were coded qualitatively by KEI; we add a quantitative layer by matching every statement to KEI’s coding scheme automatically and measuring the tone of each topic.

Key findings

  • Technical levers come first. Replacing raw materials came up in every interview, fuel switching in 24 of 26.
  • External conditions weigh heavily. Power prices, grids, subsidies and demand for green products appear in most interviews.
